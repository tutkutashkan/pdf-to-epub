    How it works

    A PDF and an EPUB disagree about what a page is. Everything below follows from that one difference.

    A fixed page becomes flowing text

    A PDF fixes every word at a coordinate on a page of a certain size. That is exactly what you want for printing, and exactly what you don't want on a small screen: the page cannot get narrower, so the text cannot get larger. Zooming just moves a magnifying glass around.

    An EPUB stores the text as text and lets the reader decide how to set it. Converting means throwing away the page geometry and keeping the words, the order and the structure.

    Scans are three different problems

    A scanned book is a stack of photographs. Whether it can become real text depends on something you cannot see by looking at it: whether anyone has already run character recognition over it, and left the result inside.

    The recognised text is usually written invisibly on top of the page image, and most converters ignore invisible text, hand you a book of photographs, and report success. This one looks for that layer and uses it, which is faster and more accurate than reading the page again.

    The margins are thrown away

    Every page of a book repeats its title, its author and its number in the margin. On paper you never notice. Extract the text and those margins become ordinary lines, so the reader flows them straight into the prose: a page number landing in the middle of a sentence, several hundred times over a novel.

    The same goes for anything stamped onto the pages afterwards. Site watermarks, download banners and the adverts baked into a free PDF all repeat in exactly the same way, so they are removed on exactly the same grounds.

    Typography is handed back to you

    A converted book should behave like one you bought. Colours and typefaces baked into the PDF are removed so your reader's own settings apply, and page margins are zeroed so the device's margin control means something. Heading sizes stay proportional rather than absolute, so they remain larger than the body text at any size you choose.

    The first page becomes the cover, so the book has a proper thumbnail in your library, and the title and author are read out of the PDF rather than left as a filename.

    Your file is deleted

    The PDF is converted and both files are removed as soon as you download the result. There is no account, nothing is kept, and nothing is sent anywhere else.

    Questions

    Why does my converted book have no text in it?

    It was almost certainly a scan. If it holds a recognised text layer this converter will find and use it. If it holds none, tick "Read scanned pages" to run OCR, or accept a fixed-layout book of page images.

    When should I turn on OCR?

    Only when a PDF contains no text whatsoever. It is skipped automatically whenever the text is already there, so leaving it on costs you nothing but rarely does anything either.

    Can I put the result on a Kindle?

    Yes. Amazon's Send to Kindle accepts EPUB, by email or through their web uploader. Choose your reader under Target device first and the book is sized for it.

    What happens to my file?

    It is converted and then deleted, along with the EPUB, as soon as you download it. Uncollected files are swept up shortly afterwards. There is no account and nothing is retained.

    Is there a size limit?

    Fifty megabytes per file. A long book is fine; conversion runs in the background and the page shows which stage it has reached.
